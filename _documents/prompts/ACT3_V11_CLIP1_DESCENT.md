# ACT III — V11 CLIP 1: THE DESCENT (OMNI FLASH)
## Google Flow / Veo — Omni Flash — 10 Second Video (Text-to-Video)

### Concept
Submerged, elemental. Camera drifts downward through the dark water of a deep silent ocean toward the outline of a submerged ancient temple far below. Shafts of warm gold moonlight cut down through the dark water from above. Fine gold sediment sinks slowly downward through the frame with real water physics — falling, not weightless. First glimpse of the temple's roof and columns below in the deep. The register is Ghibli's Ponyo underwater plus the sunken statuary of *Life of Pi*.

### V11 principle: **water physics change everything**
Cloth is heavier and drifts with current. Particles fall *downward*, not weightless. Light comes from *above* through the water. Everything reads through a subtle dark water lens — but the palette is still 85/10/5, so the water reads as dark (not cyan/teal), and the light is warm gold (not moonlight-blue). Sun-through-water, filtered to the brand palette.

### Touchstones
- **The Shape of Water** — silent submerged wonder, dark water with warm underlit tones.
- **Life of Pi shipwreck sequence** — sinking golden objects, shafts of gold light through dark water.
- **Studio Ghibli's Ponyo underwater passages** — slow water physics, particles that drift and fall.

### What this clip introduces
- **Water** (the whole environment — dark ocean water)
- **Stars** (as shafts of warm gold moonlight cutting down through the water from above — the abstracted "starlight" element)
- **Dust** (as fine gold sediment sinking downward through the frame)
- **Stone** (first glimpse of the submerged temple's roof and column tops below)

### COLOR LOCK — WATER EDITION
Deep pitch black `#000000` for the water depth, the temple silhouette, and everything past a certain visibility distance. Warm ivory `#F4F0E8` only as faint tint on the topmost water surface highlights and on the sinking gold sediment nearest the light shafts. Warm antique gold `#C9A55A` only as the shafts of moonlight coming down through the water, the temple's kintsugi gold detail, and the sinking sediment where lit. The water itself is **not tinted blue or cyan** — it reads as dark and warm-gold-lit rather than as cool ocean.

```
A silent, ultra-slow cinematic first-person forward-and-downward drift through deep dark ocean water at night. The camera moves at the pace of a held breath, on a locked forward-and-slightly-downward axis. No shake, no rotation, no zoom. Submerged, weighted, patient.

The water is deep and dark — pitch-black at any real distance, softened only by shafts of warm gold moonlight cutting down through the surface far above the camera. The moonlight passes through the water in discrete visible beams — angled, refracted, warm — spearing downward through the frame from the upper-left to the lower-right at a low angle, illuminating only what they touch and leaving the rest of the frame deep in darkness.

Fine gold sediment drifts slowly downward through the water with real water physics — small pinpoint amber-lit motes of particulate matter falling steadily through the frame at the pace of settling silt, catching the moonlight shafts only when they pass through them. Where a sediment mote is lit by a shaft it glows warm amber; where it drifts through the darker water between shafts it becomes near-invisible.

Far below the camera, at the edge of visibility in the dark water, the first outlines of a submerged ancient temple begin to resolve — the pitched roof of a dark stone structure, and the top capitals of two or three heavy stone columns rising up from a courtyard floor lost in deeper black. The temple is made of dark weathered basalt, its surface fractured by fine luminous kintsugi-style veins of antique gold that glow softly from within the stone — the only warm color on the temple itself. Everything else is dark charcoal-black silhouette against darker water.

The temple's outlines are softened by the water — atmospheric perspective through liquid, not vacuum — so the further edges of the roof and columns dissolve into deep black at a shorter distance than they would in air. Depth of field in water is short.

Between the camera and the temple, the water is filled with slowly drifting current — visible only through the way it moves the sediment and refracts the moonlight shafts. There is no scale reference other than the moonlight shafts and the far temple, so the frame feels bottomless.

Above the camera, at the top edge of the frame, a hint of the water surface — a wavering dark ceiling of gently rippling black, with the moon itself just barely suggested as a faint warm gold diffusion beyond it, never fully in frame, its light diffused through many meters of water before reaching the camera.

The material detail is exhaustive: individual gold sediment motes falling at their own rates, individual refraction ripples across the surface far above, individual kintsugi filigree lines glowing from within the temple's stone, individual weathered edges on the column capitals below.

Roughly 85% of every frame stays deep pitch black shadow — the water depth, the darker column shafts and roof shadow, the areas between moonlight shafts. Warm ivory (#F4F0E8) appears only as faint tint on the topmost water surface highlights and on the sinking sediment where nearest the moonlight, no more than 10% of the frame. Warm antique gold (#C9A55A) appears only as the discrete shafts of moonlight cutting through the water, kintsugi veins in the temple's dark stone, and the lit portions of the sinking sediment — sparse thin lines and small points only, no more than 5% of the frame.

Shot on ARRI Alexa 65, 35mm anamorphic lens with subtle horizontal flare only where a moonlight shaft crosses the near foreground, T2.8 shallow depth of field with short underwater depth cutoff, natural 35mm film grain, thick underwater atmospheric haze that reads as depth rather than as bright fog, refracted moonlight caustics dancing faintly across the near foreground, locked forward-and-downward axis, no camera shake.
```
