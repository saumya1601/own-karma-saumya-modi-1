# ACT III — V7 CLIP 3: THE COLLAPSE INTO LIGHT (OMNI FLASH — EXTEND FROM CLIP 2)
## Google Flow / Veo — Omni Flash — 10 Second Video (Image-to-Video / Extend)

### Concept
Everything in the archive begins to move toward the center. All four depth planes collapse inward at once — the cathedral-scale silk banners in the foreground fray into individual gold-lit threads, the tumbling stone tablet's kintsugi cracks release small motes of gold light, the rotating Metatron's Cube dissolves into a spiral of gold particles, the calligraphic parchments unfurl and dissolve into flowing gold script-light, and the dense mid-ground memory-field breaks apart into a slow inward-spiraling storm of gold. All of it winds itself, at the exact center of the frame, into a single small dense knot of luminous gold thread — a quiet visual echo of the golden thread that later draws itself into the OWN KARMA emblem in Act V. The knot breathes softly, holds, then loosens and comes apart. Beneath the camera, a still glassy black cosmic reflection surface reveals itself and briefly mirrors the last dissolving gold light. Dark velvet cosmic mist rises from that surface and thickens through the frame, swallowing the remaining memories, the sparse stars, and the distant heart-ember. The frame settles into deep, uniform near-black — seamless handoff to Act IV.

### V7 principle carried into this clip
Density is the whole point of V7 — so the collapse has to be *rich*, not sparse. The dissolve isn't three elements dispersing quietly. It's an entire cathedral-scale archive spiraling inward together, layer by layer, described with the same exhaustive material specificity as Clips 1 and 2. The knot itself is small and contained; everything around it is dense until the mist rises and consumes it.

### V7 principle: **the frame gets darker, not brighter**
Even at full density, every element that enters or brightens is immediately followed by an element that fades. The frame's total exposure trends downward across the shot. By the final beat, only the last dying ember of gold survives at center-frame, and then that goes too.

### Handoff notes
- **No hard timestamps in the prompt.** Flash does not reliably choreograph precise second-marks. The collapse is described as one continuous sequential progression.

This clip only has to end deep and uniformly dark.

### Why V7 is not another version of V6
- **V6** was Kubrick-minimal: one focal subject per shot, elements arriving one at a time out of a nearly-empty void, palette lock stated once briefly in the middle. That's still a valid direction and the files remain in place.
- **V7** is the deliberate opposite: **layered maximal density**, exhaustive material detail on every surface, four explicit depth planes each with its own scale and motion register, and prompts that read as a full DP-and-production-designer briefing rather than a spare screenplay direction. Both are cosmic-set, both are Flash-tuned, both hit all seven canonical Act III elements — but they represent opposite ends of the density register so the client can pick the register that fits.

### V7's density rules (why the detail actually works instead of collapsing into visual soup)
1. **Density is layered by explicit depth planes** — Clip 1 has three planes, Clip 2 has four. Each plane has its own scale, its own motion register, its own material vocabulary. Flash can render density that's motivated by depth. It can't render density that's spatially random.
2. **Every element category is described as a family, not a duplicate** — "smaller silk banners of varying size," "stone slabs of varying size," "thin gold Flower-of-Life patterns at various scales." Flash uses the variation vocabulary to break out of clone-render mode.
3. **The palette lock still holds even at maximum density** — 85% black / 10% ivory / 5% gold. Every element is dark-based with warm gold accents on the highlights only. At high element counts, the gold accents still stay sparse thin lines and small points; they don't accumulate into a bright wash.
4. **The camera lock still holds** — locked forward drift, no shake, no rotation, no orbit, no zoom. Density is in the scene, not in the camera.

### If Flash still misbehaves at high density
- **If Clip 1 or Clip 2 collapses into visual soup**: the mid-ground is doing too much for Flash. Reduce "dozens" to "roughly a dozen" in the far mid-ground description, and reduce the number of foreground elements from two silk banners + one stone tablet to just one silk banner + one stone tablet.
- **If the density spikes exposure and the frame goes brighter than 85% black**: Flash is misinterpreting "volumetric gold dust" as luminous fog. Replace "volumetric gold cosmic dust suffuses" with "a thin, sparse veil of pinpoint gold cosmic dust drifts through" — the word "veil" and "pinpoint" keep the dust from becoming an atmospheric wash.
- **If the mist step in Clip 3 renders bright**: swap "dark velvet cosmic mist" for "black cosmic smoke — dense, opaque, shadow-toned, absorbing light rather than emitting it." Flash sometimes needs the word "smoke" to break out of its fog default.
