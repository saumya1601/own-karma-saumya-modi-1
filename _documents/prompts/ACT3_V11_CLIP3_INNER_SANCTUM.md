### Concept
The camera reaches the deepest part of the flooded temple — an inner sanctum where the water is even darker and stiller. In the center of the sanctum, a single low basalt altar plinth rises from the flooded floor. On top of it: a shallow open stone bowl of even darker water (a pool within the flood — sacred water inside sea water). The camera slows and comes to rest above the altar. From above the frame — descending through a shaft of moonlight from the ruined ceiling — a single fine thread of warm gold drifts down through the water, buoyant, slower than in air. It descends slowly toward the sacred water in the bowl, catching the moonlight shaft along its length. It touches the surface. Ripples. Then dark silt begins rising from the flooded floor beneath the camera, swirling upward through the water, thickening rapidly as it rises — real disturbed-sediment behavior. It consumes the columns, banners, parchments, moonlight shafts, the descending thread, the altar, and the bowl. Frame settles into deep near-black — seamless handoff to Act IV.

### V11 principle in Clip 3: **the dissolve is rising silt**
V4 ended with rising mist. V6 ended with a knot. V8 ended at an altar. V11 ends with **rising silt** — real disturbed-sediment behavior from a flooded floor. The camera's own presence or the touching thread has disturbed something, and now the sediment rises from below in dense dark clouds. Water physics, not fog physics. This is what makes V11 the elemental version.

### The story V11 tells

They enter through a stone archway into the flooded main hall — columns veined with gold, silk banners drifting heavy in the current, waterlogged parchments floating suspended with their calligraphy dissolving into the water around them.
They reach the inner sanctum. On a stone altar, a bowl of sacred water waits — impossibly still inside the flood.
They stop.
From above, a thread of gold descends through a shaft of moonlight and touches the bowl's water.
The bowl ripples once.
Then dark silt rises from the flooded floor and takes the temple back into depth.
