### Concept
The camera keeps drifting forward through the same deep black void. This time, memories emerge from the darkness one at a time — not as a populated scene, but as apparitions that appear, drift past, and vanish. A single dark silk sail rises out of the void on the left and drifts past. A weathered stone tablet with a thin gold crack floats past on the right. A slowly rotating gold line-art symbol hovers ahead in the middle distance. Sparse warm stars begin to reveal themselves in the background. The distant ember still waits at the vanishing point. Ends on the frame that seeds Clip 3.

### V6 principles carried into this clip
- **Elements arrive sequentially, not simultaneously.** Silk enters first, then stone, then the symbol. Flash handles a scene where subjects appear one at a time far better than a scene fully populated from the first frame.
- **Each memory is huge and close** as it drifts past — cinematic scale, not a wide-shot inventory of small objects. Villeneuve's *Arrival* monolith, not a museum diorama.
- **Sparse warm stars are the only background change.** No sudden nebula, no gas clouds, no color shifts.
- **Palette lock stated once, briefly, in the middle.** No repetition top and bottom.
- **The prompt describes what enters the frame, in the order it enters.** That's a temporal instruction Flash actually respects, unlike hard timestamps.
